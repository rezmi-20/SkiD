# Landing Page Guidelines

> Defines the UX, UI, and conversion principles for marketing websites and landing pages. Every landing page should clearly communicate value, build trust, and guide visitors toward a single primary action.

---

# Landing Page Philosophy

A landing page exists to convert visitors into users or customers.

Every section should answer one question:

> "Why should I choose this product?"

Avoid unnecessary distractions.

Every section should move the visitor closer to taking action.

---

# Primary Goals

A landing page should:

- Explain the product quickly
- Build credibility
- Demonstrate value
- Address objections
- Encourage action

Everything else is secondary.

---

# The 5-Second Rule

Within five seconds, visitors should understand:

- What the product is
- Who it's for
- Why it matters
- What they should do next

If they can't, simplify the page.

---

# Recommended Page Structure

```
Navigation
↓

Hero Section

↓

Trusted By

↓

Features

↓

Product Showcase

↓

Benefits

↓

Testimonials

↓

Pricing

↓

FAQ

↓

Final CTA

↓

Footer
```

---

# Navigation

Keep navigation simple.

Include:

- Logo
- Features
- Pricing
- Resources
- Contact
- Sign In
- Get Started

Avoid large dropdown menus unless necessary.

---

# Hero Section

The hero is the most important section.

It should immediately answer:

### What is it?

Describe the product clearly.

### Who is it for?

Identify the target audience.

### Why should I care?

Explain the biggest benefit.

---

## Hero Components

Include:

- Headline
- Supporting text
- Primary CTA
- Secondary CTA (optional)
- Product screenshot
- Social proof

---

## Headlines

Good headlines focus on outcomes.

Examples:

- Hire Top Developers Faster
- Build Modern Apps Without Boilerplate
- Manage Your Team in One Workspace

Avoid vague headlines.

---

# Call to Action (CTA)

Every page should have one primary CTA.

Examples:

- Get Started
- Start Free Trial
- Book a Demo
- Create Workspace

Use consistent CTA wording throughout the page.

---

# Product Showcase

Use real screenshots whenever possible.

Show:

- Dashboard
- Mobile app
- Key workflows
- Reports
- Analytics

Avoid generic stock illustrations.

---

# Features

Focus on benefits—not just functionality.

For each feature include:

- Icon
- Title
- Short description
- Supporting screenshot (optional)

Keep descriptions concise.

---

# Benefits

Translate features into user outcomes.

Example:

Feature:

```
Real-Time Collaboration
```

Benefit:

```
Work together without endless email threads.
```

Benefits are more persuasive than technical specifications.

---

# Social Proof

Trust increases conversions.

Include:

- Customer logos
- Testimonials
- Ratings
- Case studies
- User counts
- Success metrics

Examples:

- Trusted by 10,000+ teams
- 4.9/5 average rating
- Used in 120+ countries

Never fabricate statistics.

---

# Testimonials

Each testimonial should include:

- Customer photo
- Name
- Role
- Company
- Quote

Prefer authentic customer stories over generic praise.

---

# Comparison Section

Help users understand why your product is different.

Compare against:

- Manual processes
- Competitors
- Traditional solutions

Focus on value, not criticism.

---

# Pricing

Pricing should be easy to understand.

Show:

- Monthly
- Annual
- Savings for yearly billing

Include:

- Features
- Limits
- Recommended plan
- FAQ

Avoid hidden fees.

---

# FAQ

Address common concerns.

Examples:

- Can I cancel anytime?
- Is there a free trial?
- What payment methods are supported?
- Is my data secure?
- Do you offer enterprise plans?

Reduce purchase anxiety.

---

# Final CTA

End every page with a strong call to action.

Example:

```
Ready to simplify your workflow?

Start your free trial today.

[ Get Started ]
```

Never end with a weak conclusion.

---

# Footer

Include:

- Logo
- Product links
- Resources
- Documentation
- Blog
- Careers
- Contact
- Privacy Policy
- Terms of Service
- Social links

The footer should reinforce trust.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Guide attention using:

- Typography
- Spacing
- Contrast
- Color
- Imagery

Visitors should naturally scroll downward.

---

# Images

Prefer:

- Product screenshots
- UI mockups
- Customer photos

Avoid:

- Generic stock photography
- Decorative illustrations without context

Show the product in use.

---

# Color Usage

Use:

- Neutral backgrounds
- One primary accent color
- Semantic colors for success and alerts

Avoid excessive gradients or bright color combinations.

---

# Motion

Use subtle animations to:

- Reveal content
- Highlight interactions
- Improve perceived quality

Avoid distracting effects.

---

# Mobile Experience

Ensure:

- Large touch targets
- Readable typography
- Stacked layouts
- Sticky CTA (optional)
- Optimized images

Mobile users should enjoy the same clarity as desktop users.

---

# Accessibility

Support:

- Keyboard navigation
- High contrast
- Screen readers
- Semantic HTML
- Focus indicators

Accessibility improves usability for everyone.

---

# Performance

Optimize:

- Image compression
- Lazy loading
- Font loading
- Code splitting
- Fast page rendering

A slow landing page reduces conversions.

---

# SEO Best Practices

Include:

- One H1 heading
- Logical heading hierarchy
- Descriptive meta title
- Meta description
- Alt text for images
- Structured data where appropriate

Good UX and SEO work together.

---

# Landing Page Checklist

Before launch, verify:

- Clear value proposition
- Strong hero section
- One primary CTA
- Real product screenshots
- Authentic social proof
- Transparent pricing
- Helpful FAQ
- Responsive design
- Accessible interface
- Fast loading performance

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Multiple competing CTAs
- Long paragraphs
- Hidden pricing
- Fake urgency
- Generic stock photos
- Confusing navigation
- Excessive animations
- Auto-playing videos with sound

---

# Inspiration

Study landing pages from:

- Stripe
- Vercel
- Linear
- Notion
- Framer
- Clerk
- Raycast
- Supabase

Focus on clarity, hierarchy, and conversion—not imitation.

---

# Golden Rule

A visitor should leave the hero section knowing:

- What the product does
- Why it matters
- Why they should trust it
- What action to take next

Every section should strengthen that decision.
